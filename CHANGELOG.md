## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **impala-card SDK: a header-only APDU trace, a read-only guard, and one APDU vocabulary (`com.impala.sdk.apdu4j`).** `TracingBibo` reports one `ApduExchange` per command — CLA, INS, P1, P2, Lc, SW, response length, elapsed time, outcome (`OK`, `CARD_ERROR`, `TAG_LOST`, `REFUSED`, `TRANSPORT_FAILURE`) and sequence — and the record has **no byte and no text field**, pinned by a reflection allow-list (Int, Long, the outcome enum) rather than a deny-list, because any byte-carrying wrapper would otherwise slip through; PINs, challenges, signables, signatures and credit tails therefore cannot leave the transport in any build. The hook runs inside `try/catch(Throwable)` so a failing hook never replaces the transport's own exception, and commands shorter than a header are forwarded untouched. `ReadOnlyBibo` refuses everything outside `ApduNames.READ_ONLY_INS` (the twelve non-mutating Impala commands at CLA `0x00`) plus SELECT **before the inner transport sees a byte**, failing closed on CLA `0x80`/`0x84`, unknown INS and malformed commands; it surfaces as the new `CardError.ReadOnlyRefused(command)` because `CardError.from` walks the cause chain (`ImpalaSDK.tx` wraps the transport exception) instead of laundering it into "the card operation failed". Decoration is `TracingBibo(ReadOnlyBibo(…))` so a refusal is visible in the trace. `ApduNames` names every INS and SW from `impala-card/docs/apdu.md` (`ApduNamesDocTest` pins the two against each other, with `6A82`/`6999` as the only documented exceptions until apdu.md lists them) and `ApduStats` summarises a trace. `commonMain` stays iOS-safe (no `String.format`).
- **impala-lib: `CardInspector`, local certificate checks, and an app log sink.** `CardInspector.inspect(session)` reads identity, liveness, receive state, last transfer and balance as separate `InspectionStep`s, so a blank or terminated card still yields a partial inspection with reasons; it refuses a session that is not read-only and the main thread, never throws for card refusals, and stops after a tag loss or when GET_VERSION fails (an applet that is not Impala's would give the remaining INS bytes other meanings, such as ISO DEACTIVATE FILE). The result, `CardInspection`, is deliberately not a `data` class (a generated `toString()` would print the holder name), wraps the holder name, balance and last transfer in `Masked` (`••••`), and keeps the raw public key and certificate private behind `certificateVerifiesUnder(issuerKey)`. `CardCertificates` verifies the card certificate with `java.security` (API 24+) and fingerprints keys; `CardTransportFacts` is the one place tag-layer facts are read; `CardStepLabels` turns trace exchanges into tap-progress labels; `ImpalaLibLog` mirrors the library's log lines to an app-installed sink. `ImpalaCardSession.open`/`fromBibo` gain `trace` and `readOnly` parameters (decorating before the SELECT, so the SELECT is traced and guarded) and `transport`/`aidHex`/`isReadOnly`; `CardReaderController` gains `trace` (inserted before `sessionFactory`, source-compatible with every positional Kotlin caller) and `debugInjectTapOrClose`. The debug source set gains `SimulatorUnreachableException`, a NOP `probe` reporting READY / BUSY / DOWN in a 2 s budget, and `openSession(host, port, …)`.
- **impala-android-demo: the Card Lab (Inspector · Doctor · Trace · Device), shipped in release builds.** The old NFC debug screen (`ui.nfc.NfcDebugActivity`, class name kept for the manifest, menu and demo-kit hooks) answers "is it the card, the bridge, or this phone?". It owns its own reader whose sessions are always read-only, so the Lab cannot sign, verify a PIN or write, whatever a future change to it does. The **Doctor** gives PASS / FAIL / WARN / SKIP / INFO verdicts for reader, ISO 14443-4, applet selection, applet version, personalization, the certificate under the card's and the bridge's issuer keys, the program id against the bridge's, `GET /offline/cards/{id}`, the local record, the session account and field quality; FAIL only when the card or the bridge positively says no (a 401 is SKIP "session expired"; a 404 from `GET /offline/cards/{id}` is INFO "the offline lane may not be deployed" for a card this phone registered, and WARN with the register-it remedy only for a card it did not), because a diagnostic that cries wolf trains testers to ignore it. **Trace** shows the Lab's own header-only exchanges with stats; debug builds add the last 20 production taps (64 exchanges each, bounded so a stuck loop cannot grow the ring). **Device** probes `/health`, `/version` and `/card-issuer` unauthenticated on its own client (no session, retries or redirects; 4 s connect/read, 5 s per call) with failure classes (`DNS`, `CONNECT`, `TLS`, `CLEARTEXT_BLOCKED`, `TIMEOUT`, `RATE_LIMITED`, `HTTP_<code>`), at most once per **10 s** so a tester mashing the button cannot trip the bridge's rate limiter (the Doctor reuses a probe for 60 s), and raises a **Wrong Stellar network** banner when `/health.stellar_network` differs from the build's — an APK on one network talking to a bridge on another is a money hazard nothing else detects. The holder name, balance and last transfer show only while the signed-in account is the card's account; before login (the Lab is reachable from a quiet link on the Login screen) only public card facts are shown. **Share report** has no parameter that could carry personal data. Entry points: Login, the Main overflow (Build Info now opens the Device tab), and a card's "Inspect in Card Lab". The simulated-card read and probe and the production-tap trace stay debug-only.
- **impala-android-demo: one tap-to-card sheet for every card request.** `CardTapSheet`, driven by the pure `TapStateMachine`, replaces "Snackbar, then silence": Waiting → Busy (step labels from the trace, "Contacting the bridge…") → Finishing (the bridge follow-up) → Done or Failed. Done fires only after the real outcome (`POST /card`, the submit accepted, the credit ACKed, the login succeeded), and auto-dismisses after 1.2 s. "Still working…" appears at **8 s** and Close at **20 s** — past the 15 s card APDU timeout, so by then any card command has finished or failed and only the bridge is outstanding; Close never cancels an operation, and Cancel is allowed only while waiting for the card or, on prompts that move no money, while contacting the bridge. Prompts that sign or apply money **never offer Try again** (a second attempt could sign or apply twice): they offer Resume, which only re-enters the existing recovery paths, when a recovery slot exists. An ambiguous outcome is a warning, "Outcome unknown — do not repeat", with Close only. Haptics (no vibrate permission), static end frames with animations off, and state in an activity-scoped view model, so rotation mid-tap cannot crash a detached fragment.

- **USDT0 in the conversion reserve (migration `036_add_usdt0_bucket.sql`).** Tether's USDT0 — the LayerZero OFT form of USDT, issued natively on Stellar as a classic asset since 2026-09-02 — is now a second stablecoin the reserve can hold, recognize, refund, and pay out, next to USDC. Enabled per deployment by `RESERVE_USDT0_ISSUER` (plus `RESERVE_USDT0_CODE`, default `USDT0`); unset, the seeded bucket is inert and nothing changes. The integration is a generalization rather than a second code path: the reserve now has ONE list of issuer-pinned stablecoins (`ConversionReserve::stablecoins()`) and every asset decision — deposit classification, payout and refund asset, on-chain balances, issuer burn-guards, the startup trustline audit — goes through it, so there is nothing USDT0-specific in the money paths. Identity is `(code, issuer)` only: a "USDT0" from any other issuer is a foreign token and lands in the unmatched queue, never in a bucket. Orders persist the stablecoin leg they were created with (`provider_payload.deposit_currency` for the pay-in, `hold_currency` for the payout), so a later config change never re-interprets an existing order — a pay-in in the other stablecoin is a `wrong_asset` inflow, and a payout whose asset was deconfigured freezes `on_hold` for an admin rather than guessing.
  - **Provider tickers are operator-supplied, never guessed.** Whether an order diverts to the USDT0 leg is decided by provider ticker strings (`RESERVE_USDT0_TICKERS`, empty by default); they may not collide with the tickers that already mean XLM or USDC, and tickers without an issuer are refused — both at startup, because a collision would silently route USDC orders to USDT0 and a half-configured money path must fail closed.
  - **Issuers are strkey-checksummed at startup**, USDC's included (`validate_stellar_account_id_checksum`, CRC16-XModem). The structural check accepted a mistyped issuer that looked valid on inspection, after which every real deposit classified as foreign and every payout failed at submit. The reserve is also refused as its own issuer.
  - **`POST /admin/exchange-reserve/trustlines` signs a `ChangeTrust` from the reserve seed** (admin/treasurer, rate limited, audited as `reserve.trustline_added`). This closes a pre-existing gap: the reserve seed is generate-only and its account is quarantined from `/managed-account/sign`, so there was **no supported way to add any trustline** to a generated reserve account — the enabling runbook's "fund with a USDC trustline" step was not actually possible. The startup log now shouts per missing trustline, `GET /admin/exchange-reserve` reports `asset` and `trustline` per stablecoin bucket, and the Reserve page offers an **Add trustline** button on a bucket the account cannot hold yet. The signer's build/sign/submit tail is shared between payments and trustlines, so the definitive-vs-ambiguous classification cannot drift.
  - Automated replenishment stays USDC-only (a drained USDT0 bucket is topped up through the unmatched-deposit credit path, as USDC was before 032). No testnet USDT0 deployment is published; the runbook documents the launch pubnet issuer as a value to verify, not a default.
- **SMS notification enrollment now requires the recipient to confirm a code (migration `034_notify_mobile_verification.sql`).** Adding or changing a `mobile` number on an `sms` notify record sends a 6-digit code to that number and marks the record unverified; `POST /notify/verify` confirms it and `POST /notify/verify/send` resends. Enforcement is in the dispatch query — an unconfirmed number is skipped by `dispatch_event`, so it is inert rather than merely flagged — and a database trigger clears the confirmation whenever the number changes, so verification always describes the number currently on the row. Codes live in Redis for 10 minutes, are bound to the number they were sent to, survive 5 wrong attempts before being destroyed, and are rate limited per row and per account because every send is a billed message. `POST /notify` now also validates the phone number, which it previously did not. **Existing numbers are grandfathered as verified** by the migration so applying it does not silently stop SMS for current subscribers; see the migration header to opt into strict re-verification instead.
- **Admin key import: provider credentials and custodial Stellar seeds through the bridge API (migration `033_bridge_credential_import.sql`).** An admin can now install the Changelly and OwlPay credentials, and provision custodial Stellar seeds, through `/admin/keys/*` and `/admin/stellar-seeds/*` instead of only through the deployment's environment. Gated on `KEY_IMPORT_ENABLED` (default **false**, which also stops stored credentials being read at startup, so an un-opted deployment behaves exactly as before) and on a real at-rest protection backend — the feature refuses to run with `SEED_PROTECTION_BACKEND=none`, because there is no plaintext-at-rest path and none will be added. New runbook `docs/runbooks/import-keys.md`, cross-linked from `rotate-secrets.md` and `incident-response.md`.
  - **Imports ADD by default.** Anything already in effect — including a credential the deployment supplies through environment variables, which has no stored row but is very much live — makes an import a `409` describing what is there. Replacing needs three things together: `replace: true`, `expected_fingerprint` echoing the credential being superseded — served as `replace_target_fingerprint`, since a credential can be stored without yet being the one running — and a typed `confirm_phrase` of `replace {kind} {network}`. The compare-and-swap is against the **live** stored row rather than the startup snapshot, so two admins racing cannot both win, nobody blind-replaces something they never looked at, and a credential imported earlier in a process's lifetime stays replaceable without a restart first. The phrase is deliberately **not** the fingerprint, which is on screen and copyable — naming the network is what catches the commonest operator error, the right key in the wrong environment — and the bridge *serves* the exact phrase so no client composes it and drifts. A replacement — and a revoke, more starkly, since the provider may end up unconfigured entirely — is additionally refused while non-terminal orders or replenishment cycles are running against that provider, because a provider reference means nothing to a different provider account: the reconcile poller would defer those orders forever and a cycle could be left with treasury XLM already sent and nothing able to claim it.
  - **Stored, not pushed: activation waits for a rolling restart.** Credentials resolve once per process. The fleet is autoscaled and multi-instance, so pushing into the process that served the request would update one task and leave the rest on the old key — while the response and the audit event both claimed otherwise. Every response says `effective_after: rolling_restart`, and `GET /admin/keys` reports `pending_restart` for the gap between what is stored and what each instance is running. A stored credential that fails to decrypt, fails its binding check, or no longer parses disables **that provider** and never falls back to the environment (which would silently re-activate the credential someone just replaced); it is loud — ERROR, an audit event, and `key_resolution: degraded` on `GET /health` — but never fatal, because one unreadable row must not crash-loop a fleet that also serves auth, accounts and custody. `/readyz` deliberately stays green.
  - **Sealed to the row they belong to.** Neither protection backend binds an encryption context, so a ciphertext was byte-portable between rows. Credential sets are now sealed under a header naming their kind and version, verified on decrypt, and **this closed a live pre-existing hole in `managed_seed`**: an adversary with database write access (but no KMS/Vault access) could copy the conversion reserve's ciphertext into an ordinary account's row and sign payments FROM the reserve through `/managed-account/sign` — the quarantine there keys off the account id the transplanted row no longer matches, and `sign_and_submit_payment` derives the source account from the SEED, not the row. `load_protected_seed` now asserts the decrypted seed derives the address its row claims and refuses to sign otherwise (covering legacy rows too), new seed writes carry an account-bound header (`managed_seed.format_version`), and legacy rows are re-sealed opportunistically on their next successful decrypt. Binding failures report fixed strings only — the interesting failure has another account's secret sitting in the first plaintext bytes, and `AppError` messages reach the client verbatim.
  - **The reserve's seed is generate-only, and its bootstrap gap is closed.** `init_conversion_reserve` requires a `managed_seed` row for `RESERVE_ACCOUNT_ID`, while `/managed-account/generate` refuses for exactly that account — so there was no supported way to install it. `POST /admin/stellar-seeds/generate` creates it inside the bridge, where the secret never exists in plaintext outside the process; import is **refused** for that account, because an admin-supplied reserve seed leaves the pool's signing key in a person's hands and, during bootstrap, would let whoever calls first install a key they control and capture every subsequent deposit. With the flag on, a bridge configured for a reserve it has no seed for now starts **armed but inactive** (loud, no diversion, no signing) rather than exiting, so the endpoint that repairs the condition is reachable; the quarantine stays armed throughout because it reads configuration rather than the live reserve handle, which is absent in precisely that window. A seed replacement may never change an account's Stellar address — on this bridge an account's address *is* its seed's public key, so a different seed is a different account — and Horizon is consulted to refuse a master key of weight 0.
  - **Fingerprints hash canonical parsed material, never submitted bytes.** The same RSA key handed in as PKCS#1 PEM and as PKCS#8 hex fingerprints identically, or the compare-and-swap would fabricate rotations that never happened; and RSA keys canonicalize to their **public** half, so no digest of private material is stored or displayed. Opaque API keys are hashed directly and domain-separated — an honest trade-off documented in the runbook, acceptable only because every part is high-entropy provider-issued material.
  - **Rotation is not finished until the environment variable is gone.** `GET /admin/keys` reports every variable a stored credential shadows, because `KEY_IMPORT_ENABLED=false` — the break-glass switch — reverts the fleet to whatever they still hold. Superseded versions stay decryptable for a bounded seven-day overlap so inbound webhooks signed with the previous secret still verify (OwlPay's verifier now accepts the previous secret alongside the current one, mirroring `JWT_SECRET_PREVIOUS`), then are scrubbed; revocation scrubs immediately, and names what the provider falls back to before it acts. Three new outbox events (`bridge.key_imported`, `bridge.key_revoked`, `bridge.seed_provisioned`) carry fingerprints and public identities only.
  - **Surfaces.** A new admin-UI Keys page (`keys.html` + DOM-free `keys-view.js`, vitest-covered) showing running-vs-stored per kind with type-to-confirm replacement, and warning that a key pasted into a browser is exposed to it; `impalactl keys list|import|revoke` and `impalactl stellar-seed generate|import`, which read every part from `--part-file` (the only route for a multi-line PEM), `$IMPALA_KEY_<PART>`, or a no-echo prompt — never argv — and print the bridge URL and network before sending; full openapi coverage. Provider constructors were refactored to build from a resolved credential set rather than reading the environment themselves, so a stored credential and an environment one fail in exactly the same way.

- **Conversion reserve, second wave: automated replenishment, reserved price quotes, and automated refunds (migration `032_reserve_replenish_quote_refund.sql`).** Each ships behind its own flag defaulting off, and applying 032 changes no behavior until an admin opts in twice (master switch **and** a non-zero cap — 0 means *unconfigured, refuse to run*, never "unlimited").
  - **Automated replenishment.** The pool takes XLM in and pays USDC out, so USDC drained monotonically while XLM accumulated; the USD float only drained. Two independently triggered cycle kinds now sell the accumulated float back: `xlm_to_usdc` through Changelly (fully automatic) and `usdc_to_usd` through OwlPay. Because the bridge can observe USDC leaving and a provider's transfer status but **never a bank credit**, the fiat leg books into `held` as *in transit* and only an admin confirming receipt (`POST .../replenishment/{id}/confirm-fiat`) moves it into `available` — the ledger never claims value nobody verified, and `.../write-off` is the audited escape hatch when it never arrives. Sizing reuses the admin forecast's own math (EWMA, suggested top-up) with treasury entry kinds excluded — load-bearing, because counting the bridge's own inventory moves would inflate the EWMA that sizes the next cycle, so an off-ramp would buy USDC to replace the USDC it deliberately spent. Two price guards (a floor plus a slippage bound against a reference quote) are what stop a mispriced or manipulated quote dumping the float; a chain-aware float guard proves spendability against `min(ledger, on-chain)` and skips rather than spends when Horizon is unreadable; and a cycle never starts while a customer payout may still land, since both sign from one sequence number. "One cycle in flight per kind" is a partial unique index rather than a scan, where `frozen` and `in_transit` deliberately still occupy the slot. Ambiguous sends freeze and are never resubmitted; a definitive rejection unwinds the hold cleanly. Changelly's create has no client idempotency key so an ambiguous create is abandoned (free — an unfunded swap expires), while OwlPay's is safely re-POSTed. The manual `POST .../replenishment/run` takes the watcher's own advisory lock and 409s if it is mid-tick.
  - **Reserved price quotes.** `POST /exchange/quote` with `reserve_lock` now returns a bridge-issued `reserve_quote` (id, locked `amount_to`, expiry) that locks the price **and reserves the capacity behind it**, so an order created with `reserve_quote_id` cannot fail for lack of funds. The capacity is taken once at mint and handed to the order as a zero-delta linkage entry — never released and re-taken, so no racing order can take it and the half-the-pool fraction guard is never re-evaluated against capacity already ours. A quoted order is a commitment: it never falls through to a provider at a different price. The claim is one guarded statement in which every invalid case (unknown, foreign, consumed, expired) collapses to zero rows so quote ids cannot be probed, backed by an order-term match that compares amounts in canonical minor units and payout addresses byte-exactly. A lost response replays to the same order, making `reserve_quote_id` a true idempotency key. Open quotes and open orders share one per-account cap, and the bridge refuses to start if the lock window plus the deposit window exceeds the 7200s ceiling on total price-option exposure.
  - **Automated refunds.** Deposits the reserve cannot use — arriving after expiry, underpaid, or stranded when an admin fails an order — are now returned to their payer instead of waiting on the runbook; `resolve_fail`'s response finally says where the money went. Required capturing the payer for the first time (`from`/`from_muxed` from Horizon, persisted on both the stray-inflow queue and the `deposit` journal entry). Deliberately excluded and left manual, each with a recorded `refund_skip_reason`: unknown-memo inflows (that is how ops tops the pool up — auto-refunding one would wire the float back to ops), wrong-asset inflows, `create_account` funding (the starting balance IS the base reserve), path payments (the payer parted with a different asset), muxed senders (the visible address is a shared base account), and destinations equal to the reserve or to the asset issuer (which would **burn** an issued asset). The claim is a status CAS plus a **write-ahead debit**, so the failure that matters — submitted then crashed — leaves the ledger correct with only metadata missing. Definitive rejections write a compensating reversal and retry; ambiguous outcomes freeze without reversing, because the money may be gone. Admin `reverse` is refused until 600s after the claim and only after verifying on-chain that no matching refund exists. The refund memo is deliberately distinct from an order ref, because `find_onchain_payout` reads that ref as proof a payout landed — a colliding memo would have corrupted both admin resolve actions.
  - **Shared hardening these forced.** One journal INSERT shape for the append-only ledger, widened to carry all five new linkage columns and moved behind a named-field constructor — sqlx does not check bind count at compile time, so a writer that missed a column would have failed at runtime on a money path, and three consecutive `Option<Uuid>` columns would transpose silently. A unified `ORDER_HOLD_SQL` replaces the duplicated hold lookup in `expire_one`/`resolve_fail`, which previously hard-errored when an order's hold was anchored anywhere but its own entry — every quote-backed order that expired or was resolve-failed would have aborted mid-transaction and locked its hold forever. `find_onchain_payout` now also requires the destination to match. `delete_account` additionally refuses accounts holding live price locks or unresolved refunds. Rust: `h2` moved to 0.4.19 in both the bridge and `impala-soroban/testnet-tests` (RUSTSEC-2026-0258, unbounded empty DATA frames), `quinn-proto` to 0.11.17 in testnet-tests (RUSTSEC-2026-0185, remote memory exhaustion, 7.5 high), `event-listener` to 5.4.2 (RUSTSEC-2026-0221, unsound), and the yanked `num-bigint` 0.4.7 and `spin` 0.9.8 to 0.4.8 and 0.9.9. RUSTSEC-2026-0235 (`rkyv` 0.7.46, out-of-bounds reads through `Rc`/`Arc` archives) was removed at the root rather than ignored: it was an unactivated optional dependency of `rust_decimal` that only the lockfile still recorded, and `rust_decimal` 1.43.0 drops it. `cargo-deny` was failing its `licenses` check too — the bridge crate declared no `license` field, and `impala-soroban/testnet-tests` had no `deny.toml` at all, so its empty default allow-list rejected 149 crates; both now pass, with `CDLA-Permissive-2.0` (Mozilla's CA root bundle) and `BSL-1.0` (`xxhash-rust`) added to the allow-lists. The one remaining ignore is RUSTSEC-2022-0081, `json` 0.12.4 unmaintained, which `stellar-base` 0.7.0 links through a bare `extern crate` and never calls. Go: `impalactl` moved from `go 1.26.1` to 1.26.6, clearing five reachable standard-library advisories, and `lumencli` took `klauspost/compress` 1.18.7 (GO-2026-5841) and now keys its vulncheck accept-list on `<osv>|<module>|<fixed version>` so an entry expires by itself once a fix ships. npm: the four high-severity advisories in the admin UI's dev tree (`brace-expansion`, `js-yaml`, `nanoid`, `postcss`) cleared by lockfile bump alone, and 92 lockfile entries that carried no `resolved` URL or `integrity` hash — `npm ci` was fetching them unverified — were backfilled. Scanners: `gitleaks` was failing on 12 findings, all audited and none a credential (doc comments, placeholder JWTs, test-generated RSA keys, and the vendored Oracle JavaCard SDK's own simulator key); they are suppressed per finding by fingerprint in `.gitleaksignore`, never by path, so a real secret in those same files still fails. The job now invokes the gitleaks CLI directly rather than `gitleaks-action`, which has been proprietary since v2.0.0 and requires a `GITLEAKS_LICENSE` secret for organisation-owned repositories; the scanner binary itself is MIT and free, so dropping the wrapper removes the licence obligation without losing coverage. The binary is pinned by version and verified against the SHA-256 that gitleaks publishes with the release, and the job scans both the git history and the working tree, so a secret that was committed and later deleted still fails. **The status check was renamed** from `gitleaks (secret scan)` to `secret scan (gitleaks CLI)` — update any branch-protection rule that requires the old name. Trivy now loads `.trivyignore.yaml` through a repository-root `trivy.yaml`, and the nine accepted Terraform findings moved from a `terraform/.trivyignore` that no caller ever loaded (trivy resolves an ignore file against the process working directory, and both callers scan from the repository root) to inline `#trivy:ignore:` markers on the resources themselves.

### Fixed
- **impala-android-demo: the Cards FAB works with the simulated card.** It asked for NFC first, so on the emulator (no NFC) registering a simulated card was impossible; `MainActivity.cardAvailability()` now checks a configured simulator first, and NFC off fails at once with an Open NFC settings action instead of a sheet that waits forever.
- **impala-android-demo: an unreachable simulated card no longer crashes the app or wedges the simulator.** Opening the session and injecting the tap are two guarded steps: a dead endpoint becomes a readable "Simulated card at host:port is unreachable — is :simulator:serve running?", and an injection with reader mode off closes the TCP session (`debugInjectTapOrClose`), so the one-client `SimulatorApduServer` is never left holding a dead socket. impala-lib's `TcpBibo` also closes its socket when the connect fails.
- **impala-android-demo: card login no longer drops failures or acts on stray taps.** The login screen tracked only "a tap claimed the login", so a failure thrown before `onTap` (for example the SELECT) never reached the user; it now also honours "the user asked for a tap", and taps nobody asked for are ignored and logged. Cancelling while the app waits for the bridge releases the login slot (`cardTapAbandoned`) and ignores the late result.
- **impala-android-demo: a pending-tap race and lost results.** The pending card request is taken with an atomic `getAndSet(null)`, and result callbacks check that the screen still exists; a signed transfer whose result arrives after its screen is gone stays "Ready to send" instead of being lost or submitted twice.
- **impala-android-demo: clipping, titles and system bars.** Edge-to-edge on every activity replaces the fixed 56 dp margin that clipped the FAB and Log Out; the first screen's title is correct, Up appears on nested screens, the status bar no longer shows the old violet, and the OAuth redirect activities are translucent.

With transparent bars the theme no longer decides the icon colour, and `applyEdgeToEdge` only ever forced light icons (for the login header).
